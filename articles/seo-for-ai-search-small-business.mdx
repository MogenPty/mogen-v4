Search engines are changing how people discover information. Instead of always receiving a list of links and deciding which pages to open, users may now encounter AI-generated summaries, conversational answers and related sources directly within search experiences.

For a [small business](/articles/tag/small-business), this raises an important question: does traditional search engine optimisation still matter, or is a completely different approach needed to appear in AI-powered search results?

Google's own guidance provides a useful answer. Its documentation for generative AI features explains that established SEO practices remain relevant to AI Overviews and AI Mode. The systems still rely on Google's Search index, core ranking systems and the ability to discover and understand useful web content.

This does not mean that traditional SEO guarantees visibility in AI-generated answers. It means businesses should be careful about abandoning proven foundations in favour of unverified optimisation tricks.

For South African businesses targeting customers in areas such as [Maboloka](/locations/maboloka), [Soshanguve](/locations/soshanguve), [Centurion](/locations/centurion) and surrounding communities, the practical opportunity is to make their services, expertise and useful information easier to discover and understand.

## What is changing in search?

Traditional search commonly presents a list of results that users can select and visit.

Generative [AI search](/articles/tag/ai-search) experiences can combine information from multiple sources into a response, sometimes with links to the pages that support the answer.

Google's documentation describes two important concepts behind its generative search experiences:

- **Retrieval-augmented generation:** Relevant information is retrieved from Google's Search index and used to ground generated responses.
- **Query fan-out:** The system may explore multiple related searches to gather information relevant to a broader question.

For example, a person searching for help with a business website may also need information about website costs, mobile usability, hosting, maintenance and the development process.

A search experience may explore related aspects of the question rather than relying only on the exact words originally entered.

Google's [guide to optimising for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) explains how its AI search experiences relate to existing Search systems.

The important point for a business owner is that search visibility still depends on having useful, discoverable information. A website cannot become a credible source merely by adding an AI-related label to its SEO strategy.

## 1. Do not abandon traditional SEO

A business website still needs to be accessible to search crawlers, properly structured and useful to visitors.

Google's guidance says pages must meet the technical requirements for Search and be eligible to appear with a snippet to qualify for generative AI features in [Google Search](/articles/tag/google-search).

Even when a page meets the requirements, Google does not guarantee that it will crawl, index or serve that content.

This makes the usual technical foundations important:

- Pages should return appropriate HTTP status codes.
- Important content should be accessible to crawlers.
- Canonical URLs should be implemented correctly.
- Internal links should help visitors and crawlers discover relevant pages.
- Important pages should not accidentally be blocked from indexing.
- Duplicate or unnecessary URLs should be managed appropriately.
- Page titles and headings should describe the content accurately.

These are not new AI-specific tricks. They are part of maintaining a website that search engines can understand.

A business that spends money producing AI-focused articles while its important [service pages](/articles/tag/service-pages) remain inaccessible to crawlers is solving the wrong problem.

## 2. Publish information that competitors cannot easily copy

One of the most useful parts of Google's AI search guidance is its emphasis on original, valuable and non-commodity content.

A generic article that repeats commonly available information may add very little value. A page that demonstrates real experience, explains a process or answers a difficult customer question can provide a more distinctive contribution.

Consider the difference between these two approaches.

**Generic content:**

"A professional website helps businesses grow. It should be attractive, responsive and easy to use."

**More useful content:**

"A business that receives most enquiries by telephone may need its contact details, opening hours and service information to be immediately visible on mobile devices. A complex homepage animation could make those details harder to find without helping the customer make a decision."

The second example provides context and explains why a particular design decision matters.

A business can create more useful material by documenting:

- Questions customers regularly ask.
- Common mistakes in its industry.
- The factors that influence a purchasing decision.
- How its services are delivered.
- Practical limitations customers should understand.
- Lessons learned from genuine projects.
- The differences between available solutions.

Do not invent case studies, experience, customer results or expertise. If the business has a real example, explain it accurately. If it does not, provide a clear explanation supported by credible sources.

Google's guidance describes this type of unique, helpful and people-first content as more valuable than simply producing large quantities of generic material.

## 3. Organise pages around real customer questions

A customer rarely thinks in terms of a primary keyword and six secondary keywords.

They think about a problem they need to solve.

A person considering a new website may want to know:

- What should a business website include?
- How long does development take?
- What information should be prepared before a project begins?
- What is the difference between a managed website and a self-hosted website?
- How should enquiries be handled?
- What happens after the website goes live?

These questions can inform a useful content structure.

A service page should explain the service. Supporting articles can answer related questions in greater detail. Case studies can demonstrate how the business approaches real problems.

Do not create a separate article for every possible wording of the same question. Google's guidance warns against creating unnecessary content variations primarily to manipulate rankings or AI responses.

One comprehensive, useful page may serve several closely related searches more effectively than a collection of repetitive pages.

## 4. Use clear headings and natural language

Search systems need to understand what a page is about, but that does not mean every paragraph should be written for a machine.

Use headings that describe the actual content beneath them.

For example:

- What Does Website Maintenance Include?
- How Often Should a Business Update Its Website?
- What Happens When a Domain Expires?

These headings are more useful than vague labels such as "Our Approach", "The Solution" or "More Information" when the reader needs to understand a specific topic.

Keep paragraphs focused. Explain unfamiliar terms. Use lists or tables where they make comparisons easier to understand.

Do not split a page into dozens of tiny sections simply because someone claims AI systems need content in small chunks.

Google's documentation specifically says there is no requirement to divide content into tiny pieces for AI search. It also says there is no ideal page length that applies to every subject.

Write the amount of content needed to answer the reader's question properly.

## 5. Make your website easy to crawl and navigate

AI search visibility depends in part on the underlying ability of search systems to discover relevant content.

[Internal linking](/articles/tag/internal-linking) is therefore still important.

A useful internal link connects one relevant page to another and gives the reader a clear reason to continue.

For example, an article explaining website maintenance could link to a relevant service page. A guide about [local SEO](/articles/tag/local-seo) could link to a supporting article about Google Business Profile management.

Avoid using vague link text repeatedly. A link labelled "read more" provides less context than one that describes the destination.

Google's [link best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) explain that crawlable links should generally use an anchor element with a valid `href` attribute. The documentation also explains how descriptive anchor text helps people and Google understand linked pages.

Review your website structure:

- Can visitors reach important services from the main navigation?
- Do supporting articles link to relevant service pages?
- Are there important pages with no internal links pointing to them?
- Are links implemented as real navigable links?
- Do internal links use meaningful descriptions?
- Are outdated or redundant pages creating unnecessary complexity?

A coherent website structure helps visitors explore the business and supports the discovery of relevant pages.

## 6. Do not assume special AI markup is required

One of the most common reasons businesses become interested in AI SEO is the promise of special markup that supposedly makes a website eligible for AI-generated answers.

Google's current guidance is explicit: structured data is not required for generative AI search, and there is no special Schema.org markup that must be added to appear in these features.

Structured data still has a place in a broader SEO strategy. It can help search engines understand specific information and may make eligible pages suitable for supported rich results.

But adding a large amount of schema does not guarantee AI visibility.

Use structured data when it accurately describes the page and supports an appropriate search feature. Do not add markup simply because a third-party tool recommends an AI-specific schema type that Google does not require.

Similarly, Google's guidance says that creating an `llms.txt` file does not help or harm visibility in Google Search because Google Search ignores it.

Businesses may still choose to maintain files for other tools or services that use them. They should not mistake that optional work for a Google ranking requirement.

## 7. Be cautious about AI SEO promises

The terms AEO, GEO and AI SEO are increasingly used in marketing discussions.

They can describe work intended to improve the discoverability of content in answer engines or generative search experiences. However, the labels do not establish that a particular technique is necessary or effective.

Be cautious when an SEO provider promises:

- Guaranteed inclusion in AI-generated answers.
- A fixed number of citations in AI platforms.
- Guaranteed visibility from installing a special file.
- Secret ranking signals unavailable to other practitioners.
- A specific AI ranking position within a short period.
- Results based on proprietary metrics that cannot be independently checked.

Google's documentation advises website owners to evaluate third-party SEO advice against official guidance. It also warns that third-party tools do not have access to Google's internal ranking or AI systems.

This does not mean every specialist tool is useless. Tools can help with research, reporting and workflow management. The important distinction is between a useful measurement tool and a claim that the tool can reveal or control an undisclosed ranking mechanism.

## 8. Use images and other media where they add information

Search visibility is not limited to text.

Google's AI search documentation notes that relevant images and videos can provide additional opportunities for content to appear in search experiences.

This does not mean every article needs a decorative gallery.

Choose media that helps explain the subject.

A [web development](/services/web-development) article might benefit from a genuine screenshot of a responsive layout. A product guide may need photographs showing important differences between models. A service page may benefit from an authentic image demonstrating the work involved.

Add appropriate alternative text for informative images and ensure the media does not make the page unnecessarily slow or difficult to use.

Avoid generic stock photographs that do not add meaningful context. A relevant original image is generally more useful to a reader than an unrelated photograph included merely to fill space.

## 9. Measure AI search visibility without guessing

Search Console remains an important starting point for understanding performance in Google Search.

Google's generative AI guidance points website owners to the Generative AI performance report in Search Console as a way to understand how content is discovered through its generative AI features.

This is more useful than relying entirely on third-party estimates or manually asking an AI assistant whether it mentions a business.

A practical measurement approach should consider several indicators:

| Area | What to monitor |
| --- | --- |
| Search visibility | Impressions and relevant queries |
| Organic visits | Clicks and landing pages |
| AI search | Available Generative AI performance reporting |
| Engagement | Whether visitors explore relevant pages |
| Enquiries | Whether website visits lead to useful business conversations |
| Content quality | Whether pages answer customer questions accurately |

Do not treat a rise in impressions as proof that the business is receiving more qualified enquiries. Likewise, a decline in clicks does not automatically mean the website has become less useful.

Search experiences can change how users interact with results. Evaluate the available evidence and consider how it relates to actual business outcomes.

## 10. A practical AI search plan for a small business

Most small businesses do not need a separate AI SEO programme that duplicates their existing SEO work.

A more practical approach is to improve the quality and discoverability of the website they already have.

**Step 1: Review important pages.**

Start with the homepage, service pages, contact information and articles that answer important customer questions.

**Step 2: Check technical accessibility.**

Confirm that important pages can be crawled and indexed, and that the website does not contain accidental indexing restrictions.

**Step 3: Improve content quality.**

Replace vague marketing language with clear explanations, genuine examples and useful information.

**Step 4: Improve internal links.**

Connect related pages so visitors and crawlers can discover supporting information.

**Step 5: Review structured data.**

Keep relevant markup accurate, but do not add unsupported AI-specific schema.

**Step 6: Measure performance.**

Use Search Console and available analytics to evaluate visibility, visits and enquiries.

**Step 7: Review claims from external providers.**

Compare recommendations with Google's published documentation before paying for speculative services.

For businesses targeting customers around Maboloka, Soshanguve, Centurion and other relevant areas, the same principle applies to local content: explain what the business actually offers and where it genuinely operates. Do not manufacture local pages or claims simply to create more search targets.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AI SEO different from traditional SEO?

Some terminology and search experiences are changing, but Google's guidance says established SEO practices remain relevant to its generative AI features. Technical accessibility, useful content and clear structure remain important.

### Do I need an llms.txt file for Google AI search?

Google's current guidance says Google Search ignores `llms.txt`. Creating one does not improve or harm visibility in Google Search.

### Should I add special schema markup for AI Overviews?

No special Schema.org markup is required for Google's generative AI search features. Use structured data where it accurately supports an existing, relevant search feature.

### Does publishing more articles improve AI visibility?

Not automatically. Publishing large quantities of repetitive or low-value pages does not make a website more useful. Focus on content that genuinely answers customer questions.

### Can I guarantee that my business will appear in AI answers?

No. A business can improve the discoverability and usefulness of its website, but it cannot guarantee that Google or another AI platform will select or cite a particular page.

## Conclusion

AI-powered search is changing how people discover information, but it does not make the foundations of SEO irrelevant.

Google's published guidance points back to useful content, technical accessibility, clear structure and measurable performance. Businesses should be cautious about claims that special files, artificial mentions or proprietary AI tricks can guarantee visibility.

For a small business, the best starting point is still a website that explains its services accurately, answers real customer questions and makes important information easy to find.

Build those foundations first. Then use the available reporting to understand how search behaviour and website performance evolve.

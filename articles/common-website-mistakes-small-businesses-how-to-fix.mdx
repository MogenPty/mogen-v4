A business website should help potential customers understand what the business offers, decide whether it meets their needs and take the next step. Yet a website can look attractive and still make it unnecessarily difficult for visitors to get in touch.

For small businesses serving [Maboloka](/locations/maboloka), [Soshanguve](/locations/soshanguve), [Centurion](/locations/centurion) and surrounding areas, the most useful improvements are often practical rather than flashy: clearer service information, working contact details, faster pages and a more straightforward customer journey.

Here are ten common mistakes, why they matter and how to address them.

## 1. The website does not explain the business clearly

A visitor should not have to guess what the company does or whether its services are relevant to them.

Generic headlines such as "Welcome to our website" use valuable space without explaining the offer. A better introduction identifies the service, the intended customer and the main benefit.

**How to fix it:** Review the first screen on your homepage. Within a few seconds, can a new visitor identify what you do, who you help and what to do next? Use straightforward language and avoid claims that you cannot substantiate.

A service provider working with customers in Soshanguve, for example, can explain its actual services and service coverage rather than relying on vague statements about being a leading company.

## 2. The mobile experience is an afterthought

Many customers encounter a business through a phone. Small text, crowded navigation, clipped content and buttons that are difficult to tap can make a site frustrating to use.

Google uses the mobile version of a site's content for indexing through mobile-first indexing. Its [mobile-site guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing) explains why the mobile version must remain accessible and contain the important content.

**How to fix it:** Test the actual site on phones and tablets. Check navigation, text size, forms, images, headings and contact actions. Avoid hiding important information on mobile simply to make the layout look shorter.

## 3. Pages take too long to load

Oversized images, unnecessary scripts, inefficient hosting and excessive third-party widgets can make pages slower than they need to be.

Performance is not only a technical concern. Visitors may abandon a page before reading the service information or completing an enquiry.

**How to fix it:**
- Resize images to suit their displayed dimensions.
- Use efficient image formats.
- Remove scripts and plugins that do not provide meaningful value.
- Avoid autoplay media and heavy decorative effects.
- Enable suitable caching and compression.
- Measure performance before and after making changes.

Google's [Core Web Vitals documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) explains the metrics used to assess loading performance, responsiveness and visual stability. These measurements are useful diagnostics, not a guarantee of search rankings or sales.

## 4. Contact details are missing or unreliable

A visitor who cannot easily find a telephone number, email address or enquiry route may simply choose another provider.

Outdated numbers and broken contact forms are especially damaging because the website may appear functional while silently losing enquiries.

**How to fix it:** Display suitable contact options where visitors can find them. Check that telephone links work on mobile, email addresses are current and enquiry forms reach the intended recipient. Submit a test enquiry after major website changes.

If the business serves Maboloka and nearby communities, clarify whether customers should call, message, visit by appointment or use another contact method.

## 5. The website has no clear next step

Visitors may understand the service but still be unsure what to do next. A page filled with information but no useful action leaves the customer journey unfinished.

**How to fix it:** Give each important page a primary action that fits its purpose. Depending on the business, this could be requesting a quotation, arranging a consultation, booking a service or asking a question.

Make the action descriptive. "Request a quotation" tells visitors more than a generic "Click here".

Avoid placing several competing buttons next to one another. Too many choices can make the next step less obvious.

## 6. The content is vague, outdated or copied

Service descriptions that could belong to any company do little to help customers make a decision. Old prices, discontinued services, incorrect opening hours and outdated staff details can also undermine trust.

**How to fix it:** Review the content regularly. Explain what each service includes, who it suits, how the process works and what customers should prepare. Use genuine examples where you have permission to share them.

Avoid copying competitors' text or publishing location pages that differ only by the town name. Content should answer real customer questions and accurately represent the business.

Google's [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) recommends creating useful, people-first content rather than producing pages primarily to influence search engines.

## 7. Accessibility is overlooked

Poor contrast, unlabeled fields, keyboard traps and images without meaningful alternative text can prevent people from using parts of a website.

[Accessibility](/articles/tag/accessibility) also supports usability for people viewing a screen in bright sunlight, navigating with a keyboard or using a small mobile device.

**How to fix it:** Review heading structure, keyboard navigation, focus indicators, colour contrast, image alternatives and form labels. Do not rely on colour alone to communicate errors or success.

The [W3C Web Accessibility Initiative](https://www.w3.org/WAI/design-develop/) provides guidance for designing and developing more accessible websites. Accessibility requirements depend on the context and applicable standards; do not claim compliance without an appropriate evaluation.

## 8. The design looks good but performs poorly

Large animations, video backgrounds, oversized image galleries and decorative effects may look impressive but can distract from the information customers need.

**How to fix it:** Give each design element a purpose. Use animation only when it helps communicate a change or guides attention. Check that motion does not make content difficult to read, and respect reduced-motion preferences where appropriate.

A professional website does not need to be visually complicated. Consistent spacing, typography, clear headings and relevant imagery can create a more effective experience.

## 9. Security and maintenance are ignored

A website is not finished forever when it launches. Software dependencies can need updates, forms may break, content becomes outdated and backups need to be checked.

**How to fix it:** Establish a maintenance routine. Keep supported software updated, use HTTPS, limit administrative access, remove unused components and maintain backups. Confirm that backups can actually be restored.

Businesses collecting customer details should also review how the information is transmitted, accessed, retained and protected.

## 10. Success is judged by appearance alone

A visually appealing site is not necessarily helping the business. Equally, a website with fewer visits may still be valuable if it attracts more suitable enquiries.

**How to fix it:** Decide what success means before making changes. Depending on the business, useful measures include qualified enquiries, quotation requests, bookings, completed forms and sales.

Check the entire journey from the landing page to the enquiry. If visitors reach a service page but do not contact the business, investigate whether the information, offer, trust signals or contact process needs improvement.

## A simple website review checklist

| Area | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Can visitors quickly understand the service? |
| Mobile | Is the website usable on a phone? |
| Performance | Have loading and responsiveness been measured? |
| Contact | Do all contact options work? |
| Conversion | Is the next step clear? |
| Content | Is the information accurate and useful? |
| Accessibility | Can people navigate and understand the content? |
| Security | Are software, access and backups maintained? |
| Measurement | Are useful business outcomes being tracked? |

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every website need a complete redesign to fix these problems?

No. Some issues can be corrected through content updates, image optimisation, better forms or small layout changes. A redesign is worth considering when the underlying structure or technology prevents necessary improvements.

### Do website mistakes affect SEO?

They can. Search engines need to access and understand pages, while visitors need a useful experience. However, not every usability problem has a direct ranking effect, and fixing one technical issue does not guarantee better positions.

### Should a small business prioritise design or performance?

Treat them as complementary. Start with the problems that most affect customers, such as broken contact forms, unclear service information or very slow pages. Improve visual presentation without sacrificing usability.

## Conclusion

A business website should be clear, accessible, reliable and useful. Small improvements to mobile usability, content, performance and contact journeys can make a site easier to use without automatically requiring an expensive rebuild.

Whether your customers are in Maboloka, Soshanguve, Centurion or elsewhere in Gauteng, begin by identifying the obstacles that prevent them from understanding your offer or contacting you. Fix the most important issues first, then measure the result.

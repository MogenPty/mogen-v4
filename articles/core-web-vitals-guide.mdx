Speed is no longer a nice-to-have — it's a ranking factor. Google's Core Web Vitals measure how your site *feels* to real users.

## Introduction: From Nice-to-Have to Non-Negotiable

In the competitive landscape of digital marketing, one metric has traditionally held a place as a "nice-to-have"—something we aim for but rarely touch. However, with the advent of Google's new Core Web Vitals (CWV) system, this status is being completely overturned.

For years, SEO experts and developers have relied on PageSpeed Insights and Lighthouse for useful lab diagnostics. **Core Web Vitals** complement those lab tools with real-user experience signals. They measure what actual visitors experience: the speed of loading, the responsiveness of elements, and the stability of the layout.

For a South African site, this means more than just "speed." It means ensuring your content remains accessible to visitors regardless of their device or network conditions. While many sites are fast enough for Google to rank them #1, they often fail these metrics even if they look visually appealing on a screen. Core Web Vitals ensure that the speed signals Google rewards you with higher rankings and better trust in your brand.

---

## The Landscape of Performance Metrics

Before diving into the specific metrics, it is crucial to understand what they actually measure. The new system categorizes performance into two distinct sets: **Performance Signals** (which are now ranking factors) and **Accessibility Signals**.

### 1. Performance Signals
These are the primary drivers for Google's algorithmic ranking. They dictate how quickly your site appears in search results and influence user experience metrics like bounce rate and time on page.

### 2. Accessibility Signals
These ensure that your site is usable by people with disabilities, a vital requirement for accessibility compliance (WCAG standards).

## LCP — Largest Contentful Paint

**What it measures:**
Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) determines how long the biggest visible element takes to load on screen. It specifically targets images, video, or large text blocks that appear first in a user's view.

**Why it matters:**
While 80% of users only see the top 5% of content in their viewport, LCP is critical because it dictates how quickly the entire site feels to a visitor. If the hero image takes 3 seconds instead of 1 second, the rest of the page may feel sluggish, even if other elements load instantly.

**The Fix that Beats Them All:**
A lean, well-built site wins all three. We build every Mogen site to pass Core Web Vitals by default — fast hosting, optimised assets, and zero layout shift. Maintaining a consistent low LCP is the first step toward passing these metrics without over-engineering your entire platform.

**Best Practices for South African Sites:**
*   **Compress Images:** Use WebP format instead of standard JPEG or PNG whenever possible. This reduces file size by up to 70% without losing quality.
*   **Preload Hero Images:** Ensure the primary image in the hero section is loaded immediately using
> ```
> <link rel="preload" as="image" href="/images/articles/hero.webp">
> ```
*   **CDN Strategy:** Serve assets from Content Delivery Networks (like Cloudflare or AWS) that are geographically close to South African users. This reduces latency significantly.

## INP — Interaction to Next Paint

**What it measures:**
In the new system, **INP** replaced FID (First Input Delay). INP measures the responsiveness of clicks, taps, and keyboard input across the whole page visit, tracking the time from interaction until the next paint.

**Why it matters:**
If a large JavaScript application or heavy third-party library takes 200 milliseconds (ms) to execute, the entire site feels "laggy" to the user, even if all other elements load instantly. This is often due to long-running background tasks, heavy scripts, or third-party widgets that block the page flow.

**The Fix:**
Break up long JavaScript tasks, defer non-critical scripts, and avoid heavy third-party tags. Ensure your main application logic runs quickly on every page load.

**Best Practices for South African Sites:**
*   **Defer Non-Critical Scripts:** Move scripts that don't affect the core functionality (like analytics or complex forms) into `<defer>` attributes so they run on the next paint rather than blocking the initial view.
*   **Avoid Heavy Third-Party Tags:** Limit reliance on heavy third-party widgets or plugins that may not be optimized for speed.

---

## CLS — Cumulative Layout Shift

**What it measures:**
CLS measures visual stability. It calculates the total shift in layout as a user scrolls through the page. If a large image loads and takes up more space than its previous size, the cumulative shift can exceed 0.1 (or even higher). This makes the site feel jumpy or unresponsive to scrolling.

**Why it matters:**
If your content jumps around as it loads, users will lose trust in the site's reliability. They may scroll down quickly without understanding what they are seeing. CLS is often the biggest blocker for SEO rankings because it indicates poor user experience and potential bounce rates.

**The Fix:**
Always set width and height on images and embeds. Reserve space for ads so content doesn't jump as it loads. Ensure all static elements have a defined size and position before they load.

**Best Practices for South African Sites:**
*   **Image Consistency:** Do not change the `width` or `height` of images during their lifecycle. If an image resizes, update its dimensions in your CSS or code immediately.
*   **Ad Space Management:** Ensure ad spaces are pre-empted so they don't cause layout shifts when ads load.

Choosing a website package is not simply a matter of selecting the cheapest price or the option with the most pages. A suitable website should match the way your business operates today while leaving room for sensible growth.

For a new business in [Maboloka](/locations/maboloka), an established service provider in [Soshanguve](/locations/soshanguve), or a growing company serving customers across [Centurion](/locations/centurion) and [Pretoria](/articles/tag/pretoria), the right solution depends on what customers need to do online, how much content you have and how much responsibility you want to take for maintaining the website.

This guide explains how to evaluate [website packages](/articles/tag/website-packages) and how to think about Mogen’s Seed, Sprout and Vegetative options without paying for features your business does not yet need.

## Start with the job your website must do

Before comparing packages, identify the main purpose of your website.

A website might need to:

- Explain your services and the areas you serve.
- Help potential customers trust your business.
- Display products, projects or examples of your work.
- Generate enquiries through a contact form or another clear contact method.
- Provide information customers frequently ask for.
- Support a larger digital strategy involving content, advertising or [SEO](/services/seo).

Write down the three most important actions you want a visitor to take. For example, a plumber might want visitors to understand the services offered, see the service area and request a quote. A training provider might need to explain courses, answer common questions and collect registrations.

The clearer your objective, the easier it becomes to judge whether a package is appropriate.

## Understanding Mogen’s package names

Mogen uses three [web development](/services/web-development) package names: Seed, Sprout and Vegetative. Think about them as decision points in your website journey, but always confirm the current package scope and terms before purchasing.

### Seed: establish a focused online presence

A compact, single-page website can be a practical starting point for a business with a narrow offer and limited content.

It may be suitable when you need to introduce your business, explain one main service, provide contact details and give prospective customers a clear next step. A well-organised page can combine an introduction, services, reasons to choose the business, frequently asked questions and contact information.

The important limitation is scope. If your business has many distinct services, several audiences or substantial information to publish, squeezing everything onto one page can make the experience confusing. A small initial website should still communicate clearly and make it possible to expand when needed.

### Sprout: organise a broader business offering

A multi-page website can make sense when your business needs separate pages for services, company information and other important topics.

Mogen’s Sprout package has been structured around a website of up to 10 pages, with an ongoing monthly component. Confirm the current setup charge, monthly fee, page allowance, additional-page terms and exactly what support is included before making a decision.

A multi-page structure can help visitors go directly to the information relevant to them. It can also provide a better foundation for publishing useful articles and developing focused [service pages](/articles/tag/service-pages) over time.

However, more pages are not automatically better. Each page should have a clear purpose and provide information that is useful to customers.

### Vegetative: consider your next stage of growth

As a business becomes more established, its website requirements may become more involved. It may need a larger content structure, more coordination or a different level of ongoing support.

Consider the Vegetative option only after defining those requirements and confirming its current scope with Mogen. Do not assume that a package name alone guarantees particular integrations, advanced functions or unlimited development work.

Ask for a written description of the deliverables, ongoing responsibilities, exclusions and any additional charges relevant to your project.

## Compare more than the headline price

The cheapest starting price does not necessarily represent the lowest total cost. A fair comparison should consider the complete arrangement.

| Comparison point | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Initial setup | What is included before the website launches? |
| Monthly charges | What recurring services or support do they cover? |
| Page allowance | How many pages are included, and what happens when you need more? |
| Hosting and domain | Who pays for them, and whose name is on the domain registration? |
| Business email | Is email included, optional or charged separately? How many accounts? |
| Content | Who writes and supplies the text and images? |
| Maintenance | What routine updates, checks or technical support are included? |
| Changes | Are small edits included, or are they billed separately? |
| Ownership and access | Can you access your domain, content, hosting and website files? |
| Exit arrangements | What happens if you stop the service or move to another provider? |

Ask for written answers. Two packages with similar monthly fees may provide very different levels of service.

## Once-off development or a managed arrangement?

Some businesses prefer to pay for a website build and manage hosting and updates themselves. Others prefer a recurring arrangement that reduces the amount of technical administration they must handle.

Neither approach is universally best.

A once-off project can be attractive if you have the technical knowledge, time and budget to take responsibility for ongoing maintenance. You should still plan for hosting, domain renewal, backups, security updates and future improvements.

A managed arrangement can be useful if you would rather have an agreed provider handle defined parts of the ongoing website workload. Before signing up, understand what “managed” means in practice. It should not be treated as a promise that every future change, third-party service or technical problem is included.

Compare the total cost over at least 12 months, not only the initial payment. Include any domain, email, hosting, content, support or optional service costs that apply to your situation.

## What should a good small-business website include?

Regardless of package, prioritise the fundamentals:

1. **Clear messaging.** Explain what you do, who you help and how customers can contact you.
2. **Mobile usability.** Test navigation, text, forms and contact links on a phone.
3. **Trustworthy information.** Use accurate business details, real examples and current service information.
4. **Accessible design.** Use readable text, sufficient contrast, descriptive links and properly labelled forms.
5. **Reliable contact routes.** Make it easy to enquire without forcing visitors to search for a phone number or email address.
6. **Search-friendly structure.** Give pages descriptive titles, useful headings and meaningful internal links.
7. **Performance.** Avoid unnecessarily large images and features that make pages slow or difficult to use.
8. **Maintainability.** Know who is responsible for updates, renewals, backups and fixes.

These fundamentals matter more than decorative effects that do not help customers understand or use your website.

## How to choose based on your business stage

A simple decision framework can help:

- **You need a professional starting point:** consider a compact website if your offer is straightforward and the content fits comfortably on one page.
- **You offer several services:** consider a multi-page structure that lets each important service have its own explanation.
- **You expect regular content changes:** ask how updates are handled and what ongoing support costs.
- **You need custom functionality:** describe the workflow first and ask whether it is included, requires an add-on or needs a separate quote.
- **You are unsure what you need:** prioritise a conversation about business goals and scope before committing to a package.

Businesses around Maboloka, Soshanguve, Centurion and Pretoria should also consider their actual customer journey. A local service business may need strong service descriptions and convenient contact details, while a business serving clients nationally may need a broader content and lead-generation strategy.

## Questions to ask before committing

Before approving a quote, make sure you can answer these questions:

- What exactly will be delivered at launch?
- What do I need to provide, and by when?
- Are domain registration, hosting and email included?
- Who owns the domain and has administrative access?
- How are revisions and additional work charged?
- What happens if I need more pages later?
- What ongoing maintenance is included?
- How will the site be tested on mobile devices?
- What are the renewal, cancellation and transfer arrangements?
- Which results can reasonably be measured after launch?

A trustworthy proposal should make the scope and responsibilities understandable without requiring you to guess what the package name means.

## Final recommendation

Choose the smallest package that genuinely meets your requirements, not the smallest package available regardless of fit. If your business needs only a focused introduction and a clear enquiry route, a compact website may be enough. If you have several services and want room for useful content, a multi-page website may be more appropriate.

Most importantly, confirm current prices and inclusions directly before purchasing. Package terms and promotions can change, and your written quotation should take precedence over assumptions based on an older article.
